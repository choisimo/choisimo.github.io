# Dark City (1998) - A Science Fiction Masterpiece Read Through Existentialist Philosophy

## Film Overview

**Dark City** is a science fiction noir film presented by director Alex Proyas in 1998, a hidden masterpiece that dealt with the innovative concept of simulation reality one year before The Matrix. However, the film's true value lies not in its mere SF imagination, but in how it **excellently implements the core propositions of existentialist philosophy in cinematic language**.

## Sartre's "Existence Precedes Essence" - John Murdoch's Awakening

### Humans as Beings Without Essence

Jean-Paul Sartre argued that humans exist first, then create their own essence. Dark City's John Murdoch is a perfect embodiment of this proposition. **Having awakened with lost memory, he has neither past nor identity**. Only his present choices define him.

> "Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains" - Sartre

When Murdoch breaks free from the aliens' manipulation, he gains what Sartre called **absolute freedom**. He defines his existence through pure acts of choice, not depending on implanted memories or environment.

## Kierkegaard's Anxiety and Choice - The Terror of Tuning Time

### Dizziness Before Possibility

Søren Kierkegaard defined anxiety as "dizziness before possibility." The "tuning" that occurs every night at 12 o'clock in Dark City is a visual implementation of this existential anxiety. **Humans freeze before the possibility that everything can change**.

### The Agony of Choice and Responsibility

Kierkegaard's emphasis on **choice as a solitary individual** is starkly revealed when Murdoch finally reconstructs the city. He creates a new world through his will alone, not depending on any external standards or moral laws.

## Camus's Absurdity and Rebellion - The Meaning of Endless Experiments

### Sisyphus's Rock and Tuning's Repetition

Albert Camus's philosophy of the absurd remarkably coincides with Dark City's core setting. **The nightly repetition of tuning is an absurd repetition like Sisyphus rolling his rock**. The aliens' experiments have neither clear purpose nor endpoint.

### The Rebellious Human

> "The only thing an absurd man can do is rebel" - Camus

Murdoch's awakening and resistance is a perfect example of what Camus called **rebellion against the absurd**. He doesn't accept fate but fights against absurd situations. What matters here is not victory but rebellion itself.

## Heidegger's Dasein and Authentic Being

### Beings Buried in Everydayness

What Martin Heidegger called **beings buried in everydayness** precisely describes Dark City's ordinary citizens. They don't question even when their memories change every night, living as **Das Man (the anonymous crowd)**.

### Awakening Before Death

Heidegger said we become authentic beings when we become conscious of death. When Murdoch faces the crisis of death as a serial murder suspect, he finally becomes **his authentic self** and begins pursuing truth.

## Nietzsche's Self-Creation and Will to Power

### The Death of God and Creation of New Values

Friedrich Nietzsche's declaration of "the death of God" appears as **the collapse of existing order** in Dark City. When the fake reality created by aliens crumbles, Murdoch becomes what Nietzsche called **a creator of new values**.

### Amor Fati and Eternal Recurrence

Nietzsche's concept of **amor fati** is implemented when Murdoch finally recreates the city. He accepts even past pain and wounds, showing the courage to start everything anew.

## The Film's Core Message from an Existentialist Perspective

### True Freedom Comes from Choice

Dark City shows that **freedom is not given but seized**. Murdoch's journey starkly reveals the human condition of being "condemned to be free" as Sartre described.

### Anxiety and Solitude as Driving Forces of Growth

The **existential anxiety** emphasized by Kierkegaard and Heidegger is a prerequisite for awakening in Dark City. Only Murdoch could endure this anxiety and reach the truth.

### Creating Meaning in an Absurd World

Like Camus's philosophy, the film celebrates **human greatness in creating meaning in a meaningless world**.

## Modern Interpretation: Existentialism in the Digital Age

### Similarity Between Algorithms and Tuning

The **manipulation by social media algorithms** we currently experience is remarkably similar to the tuning in the film. Our preferences and perceptions are being manipulated by invisible forces.

### The Problem of Digital Identity

SNS profiles, digital personas, etc., raise essentially the same problems as the **implanted memories** in Dark City. The existential question "Who am I really?" is more urgent than ever.

## Conclusion Reinterpreted Through Existentialist Philosophy

Dark City is more than a simple SF thriller - it's **a masterpiece that cinematically implements the essence of 20th-century existentialist philosophy**. Core concepts of existentialism such as Sartre's freedom, Kierkegaard's anxiety, Camus's rebellion, Heidegger's authenticity, and Nietzsche's self-creation permeate throughout the film.

Particularly, **the exploration of authentic existence** approaches us with an even more urgent message as we live in the current digital age. In an era of virtual reality and artificial intelligence, the existential question "Who am I?" remains valid, and Dark City serves as a compass for the journey to find that answer.

**True freedom is not given, but something we choose for ourselves by enduring anxiety and solitude** - this message from the film perfectly aligns with the core of existentialist philosophy. This is precisely why Dark City should be evaluated not as mere entertainment but as an artwork demanding philosophical reflection.
