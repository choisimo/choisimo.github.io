While researching the current state of AI adoption in the Middle East for a recent class, I realized that technology isn't simply bringing convenience, but is creating completely new conflict structures.

## AI's Two Faces - Hope and Weapon

AI has really complex meanings in the Middle East.

### GCC Countries' Post-Oil Strategy

For **Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC)** countries, AI is a key card for preparing for the post-oil era:

- **Saudi Arabia**: Building massive AI smart city 'NEOM' with 'Vision 2030'
- **UAE**: Appointed world's first AI minister in 2017, aims to convert 50% of government services to AI platforms

They're pouring vast wealth earned from oil into AI. In a way, it's a natural choice.

### But Also a Strategic Weapon

However, AI isn't just an economic tool but also operates as a **strategic weapon**. Particularly, the technology gap between Israel and the Arab world is creating new arms competition.

## Reality of Technology Hegemony Competition

### Israel's Overwhelming Advantage

There's a reason Israel is called 'Silicon Wadi':

**Unit 8200 Ecosystem**
- Perfect military-civilian-government cooperation system established
- Military AI technology naturally transfers to civilian sector
- Military surveillance AI like 'Blue Wolf', 'Pegasus' in actual combat use

Looking at specific examples, startups created by Unit 8200 alumni form the core of Israel's AI ecosystem.

### GCC's 'Capital-Led' Pursuit and Limitations

Meanwhile, GCC countries have money but structural limitations:

- **'Imported Innovation' Dilemma**: Easy to buy technology but lacking in-house development capabilities
- **Top-down Approach**: Government-led promotion makes innovation ecosystem formation difficult

Eventually, an asymmetric structure emerged where Israel competes with **technology** and GCC with **capital**.

## Shadow of Digital Authoritarianism

The most concerning aspect of AI adoption is the strengthening of **digital authoritarianism**.

### Arrival of AI Surveillance Society

**Behind Smart Cities**:
- Building comprehensive surveillance systems under the pretext of citizen convenience
- Every move of individuals monitored by AI
- 'Algorithmic Apartheid' - new discrimination created by technology

### Algorithm Bias Problem

Particularly serious is the **cultural bias** problem:
- Arab culture gets distorted by directly adopting Western-centric AI models
- Linguistic discrimination occurs due to lack of Arabic datasets
- Conflicts between Islamic ethics and AI technology

## New Security Threats

What happens when AI falls into the hands of terrorist groups? It's scary just to imagine.

### Terrorist Groups' AI Utilization

- **Low-cost High-efficiency Autonomous Attack Systems**: Automatic attacks by controlling drones with AI
- **Intelligent Propaganda**: Creating personalized propaganda with AI, effective brainwashing
- **Deepfake Utilization**: Creating social chaos with fake news

### Paradoxical Situation

Ironically, **counter-terrorism AI** sometimes becomes a tool for human rights violations. It justifies citizen surveillance under the pretext of preventing terrorism.

## Lessons We Can Learn

Looking at these cases, we can derive several lessons:

### 1. Illusion of Technology Neutrality

While technology itself is said to be neutral, in reality it produces completely different results depending on **who, how, and why it's used**.

### 2. Economic Motivation vs Political Reality

GCC countries' economic diversification efforts are rational, but unexpected problems arise when they clash with existing political structures.

### 3. New Conflicts Created by Technology Gap

A different dimension of **technology gap** from traditional military or economic power gaps is becoming a new conflict factor.

## Thoughts on Solutions

### Need for Multilateral Cooperation

To ease technology hegemony competition, **regional-level cooperation systems** are needed. Rather than developing technology while checking each other, cooperation centered on common interests (terrorism prevention, economic development, etc.) would be more effective.

### Responsible AI Governance

**Ethical AI usage standards** should be established and shared at the regional level. Especially:
- Ensuring transparency in surveillance technology use
- Algorithm bias verification systems
- Citizen privacy protection mechanisms

### Inclusive Talent Investment

Ultimately, technology is created and used by people. Educational investment and opportunity expansion are needed so **people from diverse backgrounds** can participate in AI development.

## Conclusion

Looking at Middle East AI adoption cases, I realized once again that technological development doesn't always bring positive results.

I particularly felt the danger of **technological determinism**. The naive belief that "everything will automatically improve when AI develops" is dangerous. Without **social consensus** and **institutional support** on how to use technology, it can actually worsen existing problems.

Whether the Middle East can achieve sustainable peace and prosperity through technological development will likely depend on what choices they make.
